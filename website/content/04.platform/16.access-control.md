## Overview

Every request a client makes to a published service, and every MCP tool call, is checked once where it enters the system: the caller must hold the permission the function's contract names, on the object the request names. Permissions live in [OpenFGA](https://openfga.dev) (see [Configuration](/platform/configuration#authorization-engine)): a **role** bundles permissions, a **grant** binds a role to a user or a group on a resource, and the role's permissions reach that resource and everything inside it. Where a grant is made decides what it covers.

The engine holds one store for the system, where the organizations' developer-side graph and the system's own staff live, and one store per application, where the application's users, tenants and data grants live. Each store runs a model generated from the declarations on the services it serves, so a permission exists because a service declares a function that needs it.

| Store | Engine name | Who is checked there | Managed through |
|---|---|---|---|
| System | `kinotic-system` | An organization's members and machines, delegates acting for them, system users and the system's machines | `PermissionService` (an organization's grants), `SystemAccessService` (the system's) |
| Application | `kinotic-app-` followed by the 128-bit hash of `<organizationId>.<applicationId>` | The application's users and machines | `ApplicationAccessService` |

## Subjects

A check names the caller as a `user` of the store, chosen by `AuthzGraphUtil.authzUser`:

| Caller | User in the store |
|---|---|
| An organization user | Itself: `user:<organizationUserId>` |
| A system user | Itself: `user:<systemUserId>` |
| A delegate (the CLI, an MCP host) | The user its owner is in the delegate's scope, within its type's ceiling |
| A machine, of an organization or of the system | Itself: `user:<machineId>` |
| An application's user or machine | Itself: `user:<identityId>`, in the application's store |

A delegate exercises its owner's permissions only within the ceiling of its `DelegateType`, which the gateway applies before it asks the engine: the CLI reads and edits projects and entity definitions, which is what synchronizing a project needs, and is refused everything else its owner holds; an MCP host exercises everything its owner holds but the permissions deciding who may act, `can_manage_access`, `can_manage_machines` and `can_manage_members`. A delegate naming no known type is refused every checked function. The listings that show a caller what it may see, such as `ApplicationService.findAll` and `PermissionService.listAccessible`, apply the same ceiling, so the CLI lists only the applications holding a project or an entity definition it reads.

An organization user belongs to one organization, so a person in several organizations is a different organization user in each, holding only what that organization granted. A **group** of an organization collects its members, and a grant to a group reaches whoever is in the group at the time of a check.

## Permissions

A permission is named for the resource type it is about and an action, `<type>_<permission>` in the model: `project_can_edit`, `entity_definition_can_delete`, `organization_can_manage_members`. The derived permissions imply each other wherever a type has them: `can_delete` implies `can_edit`, which implies `can_read`. A permission a service names itself, such as `can_report`, stands alone unless its declaration says what it implies.

The permissions an organization's services declare on the organization itself are what its members manage rather than build:

| Permission | Needed by |
|---|---|
| `can_read_members`, `can_manage_members` | `MemberService`: the members, pending members and invitations |
| `can_read_machines`, `can_manage_machines` | `MachineService`: the machines of the organization's applications |
| `can_read_integrations`, `can_manage_integrations` | `GitHubAppInstallationService` |
| `can_read_access`, `can_manage_access` | `PermissionService`, and the sign-in providers of `OwnedOidcConfigurationService` and `OwnedPasswordConfigurationService` |
| `can_read_deployments`, `can_manage_deployments` | The history, restart and removal of a microservice or UI deployment |
| `can_read_telemetry` | `LogService` and `TelemetryService` |
| `can_read_jobs` | `JobMonitoringService` |

An application, a project and an entity definition carry the derived `can_read`, `can_edit` and `can_delete` of the services acting on them; an application also carries `can_read_access` and `can_manage_access`, which `ApplicationAccessService` needs, and `can_register_services`, which a runtime registers the application's services with. The full catalog of the system store is whatever `PermissionService.findPermissions` returns, by type: adding a function to a published service adds its permission once the service's contract is in the directory.

## Roles

A role bundles permissions, and its id is `<type>.<level>`. The model defines, for every resource type of a store:

| Role | Holds |
|---|---|
| `<type>.viewer` | The type's reading permissions: `can_read` and every `can_read_<something>` |
| `<type>.editor` | Every permission of the type but `can_delete` and the permissions deciding who may act, `can_manage_access`, `can_manage_machines` and `can_manage_members`, so an editor cannot grant itself a role or act as a machine holding more than it does |
| `<type>.admin` | Every permission of the type and of every type inside it |

The system store adds three roles of its own, and the services declare the rest:

| Role | Holds |
|---|---|
| `application.developer` | Every permission of every type inside an application, and none of the application's own |
| `system.maintainer` | The system's own permissions but `can_manage_access`, `can_manage_machines` and `can_manage_members`, and the reading permissions of everything on the system |
| `system.support` | The reading permissions of the system and of everything on it |
| `application.runtime` | `application_can_register_services`, declared by `ApplicationServiceDirectory` |
| `vm_node.registrar` | `vm_node_can_register_node`, declared by `VmNodeOrchestrationService` |
| `vm_node.agent` | `vm_node_can_report` and `vm_node_can_read`, declared by `VmNodeOrchestrationService` |

`organization.admin` is therefore an organization's administrator, holding everything in it. A role that would bundle nothing is not defined, so a type with no reading permission has no viewer. Built-in roles are named for display from their id, `project.editor` as `Project Editor`, and cannot be changed.

### Where a role can be granted

Every role is homed at the type its id starts with: `project.editor` at `project`, `system.support` at `system`. A role bundles only permissions of its home and of the types inside it, and is granted only on its home or on a type above it, within one tier:

| Tier | Managed through | Roles homed at | Granted on |
|---|---|---|---|
| System | `SystemAccessService` | `system`, `vm_node` | The system |
| Organization | `PermissionService` | `organization`, `application`, `project`, `entity_definition` | The organization and everything inside it |
| Application | `ApplicationAccessService` | Every type of the application's store | The application, its tenants and definitions, and a definition within a tenant |

A tier ends where a lower one begins: the system grants none of an organization's roles, so `project.editor` on the system does not reach every customer's projects, and an organization grants none of a tenant's. Cross-organization access for staff comes only from the system's own roles. Within a tier a role homed lower can be granted higher and reaches down, and one homed higher cannot be granted lower: `organization.admin` on a project, or `application.admin` on a tenant, is refused.

The engine enforces this itself. Each type `<type>` of a store has a role type `<type>_role`, holding relations only for the permissions its roles may bundle, and a binding type `<type>_role_binding`, which accepts only the role types homed at `<type>` or inside it within its tier; a resource accepts only its own type's bindings. A role is the object `<home>_role:<role id>`, such as `project_role:project.editor`, and a grant on a project is a `project_role_binding`. A tuple placing a role at the wrong level fails at the write. `findRoles` of each tier lists only that tier's roles, each with its home as `resourceType` and the types of the tier it may be granted on as `grantableOn`, and `findPermissions` the permissions of the organization's tree.

### Custom roles

An organization defines roles of its own from the catalog with `PermissionService.saveRole`: a name, a description, its home (`resourceType`: the organization, an application, a project or an entity definition) and the model names of the permissions it bundles, which must be of the home or of a type inside it. A custom role's id is `<home>.<uuid>`, and its home does not change once it is created. Changing a role's permissions changes every grant of it at once. `deleteRole` deletes a role once no grant holds it.

## Groups

`saveGroup` creates or renames a group of the organization, which starts with no members; `addGroupMember` adds a member of the organization to it, `removeGroupMember` takes one out, and `findGroupMembers` lists them. `deleteGroup` deletes a group once no grant is made to it.

## Grants

`PermissionService.grant(subject, roleId, resource)` gives a role, built in or the organization's own, on the organization or on an application, a project or an entity definition inside it, to a member (named by its organization user id), to one of the organization's own machines, or to a group. The subject holds every permission the role bundles on the resource and on everything inside it:

| Wanted | Grant |
|---|---|
| Sally can modify project A but not B | `project.editor` on project A |
| Marcus can work with every project of Atlas CRM | `application.developer` on the application |
| Priya can work with every project of every application, including ones created later | `application.developer` on the organization |
| QA can view every project of the organization but edit none | `project.viewer` on the organization, with the group as the subject |

`revoke(resource, grantId)` takes a grant back where it was made, refusing the last grant of `organization.admin` on the organization held by an enabled organization user so the organization keeps an administrator who can sign in. `findGrants(resource)` lists the grants that reach a resource: those made on it, then those made on each ancestor up to the organization, each naming the resource it was made on.

### Who holds what from the start

A member added to an organization holds no role until one is granted. Grants are also written by the system itself:

- **Sign-up** binds the founder as `organization.admin` on the organization it creates.
- **Seeded organizations**: when the management server starts, `OrganizationGraphBootstrap` places every organization not yet on the system in the graph: its enabled organization users as its members, its `createdBy` organization user as `organization.admin` while that user is an enabled member, and the organization on the system, written last. An organization on the system is left as it is, whatever was granted or revoked since. The same sweep provisions the store of every application that has none.
- **Deployments**: every time a deployment issues a workload's credential, `ProjectDeployIdentityService` binds the machine to what its workload does. Both the sync machine and each runtime machine are bound as `project.editor` on the project; the sync machine as `entity_definition.editor` on the project's application; each runtime machine as `application.runtime` and `entity_definition.admin` on the application.
- **Node registration**: a vm-manager's machine registers a node with `vm_node.registrar` on the system, and the registration binds it as `vm_node.agent` on that node, which its heartbeats and status reports are checked for. Deregistering the node revokes every grant made on it.
- **System staff**: when the system server starts and no one holds `system.admin` on the system, `SystemGraphBootstrap` places every node on the system, binds every enabled system machine as `vm_node.registrar` and, last, every enabled system user as `system.admin`. A system with an administrator is left as it is.

Removing a member from an organization deletes the organization user, which takes it out of every group and revokes every grant made to it, so a person added again starts with none. Disabling an organization user takes it out of the organization's members, and enabling it puts it back. Deleting a system user, a machine or an application's user does the same in every store it is held in.

Deleting an organization, an application, a project or an entity definition revokes every grant made on it, and deleting an application deletes its store, so one created again under the same id starts with none. An application, a project, an entity definition, a tenant, a named query or an invite template keeps its application: a save naming another application than the stored record's is refused. A named query definition's id is its entity definition's, `<organizationId>.<applicationId>.<entityDefinitionName>` lowercased, which its writes are checked on.

## Reading access

`listAccessible(type, permission)` returns the ids of the resources of a type the caller holds a permission on, through any grant that reaches them. The listings read it before they query, unless the caller holds the permission on its organization, and so on every resource of the type, which the engine's bounded listing is then not asked for:

- `ApplicationService` lists, counts, searches and finds by id only the applications the caller may read and those containing a project or an entity definition the caller may read.
- `ProjectService` lists, counts, searches and finds by id only the projects the caller may read and those containing an entity definition the caller may read, also within one application.
- `EntityDefinitionService` finds by id only a definition the caller may read.

A member granted `project.editor` on one project therefore sees that project's application and, inside it, that project alone. An application, a project or a definition the member may not see is absent from a listing and answered as `null` by id, as an id nothing has is.

`explain(subject, permission, resource)` answers whether a member or a group holds a permission, named by its short name such as `can_edit`, on a resource, and names the grants it holds the permission through. It asks the engine for a consistent answer, so it reflects a grant made or revoked a moment ago. A grant explains a permission its role bundles or one a bundled permission implies, so an editor's grant explains reading. A permission held through nothing a grant explains, such as being a member of the organization, is allowed with no grant named.

## Enforcement

The STOMP gateway checks each service request a client sends, and the MCP endpoint each tool call, with the same `RequestAuthorizer`, after the zone rules have admitted the destination and before anything is sent. A refusal answers the caller with an `AuthorizationException` reading `Not authorized`, and a tool call gets it as the tool's error result. The reason, such as the permission and the object refused, is logged at `WARN` by the server that refused it, and a server with `kinotic.debug` on, as the `development` profile sets it, answers `Not authorized: ` followed by the reason. Calls between services inside the system carry the caller's participant and are not checked again, so a request is authorized by the check at its entry.

The check is the one stored with the function's contract in the service directory. The authorizer reads a service's contract once and keeps it until the directory announces the contract written again, and for five minutes at most. A function is served without the engine only when its contract marks it unchecked; a function no contract covers is refused: a service with no entry in the directory, a function its contract leaves out, or one it neither checks nor marks. A check the caller's store has no model for is refused too: a permission of a type another store models, as when an organization's member calls a service an application publishes, or of a service whose model the reconciler has yet to write.

The object is read from the request: by the parameter's position in a client's positional JSON body, with a parse that stops once it has read the id, or by the parameter's name from a tool call's arguments, and down a property path such as `entry.applicationId`. A reference to the caller's scope, such as `{@organizationId}`, is read from the caller. A request is refused without asking the engine when it names no value its check needs, when its body is not JSON, when an object the path descends into names a property twice, or when the value read is no valid id.

- **Scope levels.** A check on the caller's tenant, application or organization is made on the innermost of them the caller has, and on the system for a caller with none: an organization member asked for a listing within an application is checked on the organization, and a system user on the system. Every ancestor carries the permissions of everything below it, so the check keeps its meaning there.
- **Qualified ids.** An application's and a project's id is unique only within its organization, so the stores know each as `<organizationId>.<id>`, `application:acme.crm` for the application `crm` of `acme`. The authorizer qualifies the id a request names with the caller's organization, and refuses a caller outside every organization a check on one. `PermissionService` and `ApplicationAccessService` take and answer resources by the id the organization knows.
- **Stores.** An application's user or machine is checked in its application's store; every other caller in the system's store.
- **Consistency.** A check answers from the engine's caches, which may lag a change by a moment. A function declared `consistent`, such as removing or disabling a member or rotating a machine's secret, is answered from the stored relationships. Each server keeps the model version a store runs for 30 seconds before reading it again, and the check names that version.

A server reports its `system-authorization` health check failing until the system store runs a model, since until then every checked request is refused.

## Declaring what a function does

A service declares the resource type its functions act on once, with `@AuthzResource`, and the check of each function, a permission, a type and an id, is derived from its name and parameters when the contract is registered. `@AuthzCheck` states a part the derivation gets wrong or cannot read, and `@AuthzUnchecked` marks a function served with no check. The same declarations exist as Java annotations on a `@Publish` interface and as TypeScript decorators on an application's service, and the system derives both the same way; the [Decorators Reference](/apps/reference/decorators#authorization-decorators) lists their attributes.

| Function | Derived check |
|---|---|
| Name starts with `find`, `get`, `count`, `search` or `list` | `can_read` of the service's type |
| `save`, `update` or `set` | `can_edit` |
| `delete` or `remove` | `can_delete` |
| `create` | `can_edit` of the service's type, on the parent |
| Any other verb | Registration fails unless the function declares a permission |
| A string parameter named `id` or `<type>Id` (`entityDefinitionId` for `entity_definition`), else an object parameter with an `id` property | The check is on that object: `{id}`, `{projectId}`, `{entry.id}` |
| No id of the type | The check is on the parent, named by a `<parent>Id` parameter or property, else by the caller's scope (`{@organizationId}`, `{@applicationId}`, `{@tenantId}`), else `kinotic` for the system |

A service whose functions act on one object the request does not name declares it once with `resourceId` on `@AuthzResource`, as the organization's services name `{@organizationId}`; a service whose functions all need one permission declares it once with `permission`, as the workload services name `can_manage_workloads`, and a function declaring a permission of its own keeps it. A function inherited from a base interface, such as `findById` of a CRUD service, is redeclared on the service's interface to carry a check of its own, or marked unchecked where the service answers it by what the caller may see, as the application, project and entity definition services' `findById` do. A resource service's function that derives no check and declares none fails the service's registration, so no function of a resource service goes unchecked by accident. A `@Publish`ed Java service declaring neither a resource nor the unchecked mark fails its registration, so the server does not start; a service an application's runtime registers with neither has no check for any function, and every request to it is refused.

`@AuthzResource` also declares the type's own roles, each an `@AuthzRole` with an id `<type>.<level>` and the short names of permissions the type's functions need. The generator refuses a model in which a declared role bundles a permission no function of the type needs, or redefines a role the model defines, and a permission whose model name is longer than 50 characters.

## Stores and their models

The model of a store is generated from the contracts in the service directory that belong to it: the system's from the system's own services, an application's from the system services declared on `entity_definition` or `tenant` and the services the application's runtimes registered. Both carry the kernel types `user` and `group`, and for each resource type its `<type>_role` and `<type>_role_binding` (see [Where a role can be granted](#where-a-role-can-be-granted)); the system's also `system`, `organization` and `application`, and an application's `application`, `tenant`, `entity_definition` and `tenant_definition`, whose `tenant_definition_role_binding` grants only the roles of a definition's rows.

Each store has a record in `kinotic_authz_store`, `system` for the system's and `<organizationId>.<applicationId>` for an application's. When the directory writes a contract that changed, it renews the intent of the store the contract belongs to, and the system server's reconcile master runs `AuthzStoreReconciler`, which generates the model, writes it as a new version only when it differs from the one the engine runs, and brings the built-in roles' permissions in step with it. The master also reconciles every store when it starts.

The system's store is created before the servers start (`openfga-init` in docker-compose, the kinotic chart's `openfga-store` Job in Kubernetes) and its record by the migration. An application's store is provisioned when the application is created: the application is placed under its organization in the system's store, its store is created with the kernel model and its roles, and its record is written. When the management server starts, `OrganizationGraphBootstrap` provisions the store of every application that has none, placing the projects, entity definitions and users the application already holds. Deleting an application deletes its store with everything in it.

## The system's own tier

The system is the object `system:kinotic`, the root every organization sits under, and the services published in `system-api` are checked on it and on what is on it. Its own permissions are `can_read_cluster` and `can_manage_cluster` for the cluster, the ledger of watched records and the start of a system job, `can_read_jobs` for the list of system jobs, `can_read_integrations` and `can_manage_integrations` for the LLM configurations, `can_read_members` for the system users, `can_read_machines` and `can_manage_machines` for the system's machines, `can_read_workloads` and `can_manage_workloads` for the workloads and the restart and removal of deployed microservices and sites, and `can_read_access` and `can_manage_access` for the system's grants. A read across every organization, such as `SystemOrganizationService.findOrganizations`, is checked for `organization_can_read` on the system; a read of one organization, its applications, projects or members, on that organization, which a grant on the system reaches through the graph.

A system user calling a service of an organization's management plane is checked on the system wherever the check names the caller's organization, which a system user has none of. The telemetry services read any organization's tenant for a system user holding `organization_can_read_telemetry` on the system.

`SystemAccessService`, published in `system-api`, manages the system's grants: `findRoles` lists the roles homed at the system or at a node, `findGrants` the grants made on the system, `grant(subject, roleId)` makes one to a system user or a system machine, named by its identity id, `revoke(grantId)` takes one back, refusing the last grant of `system.admin` held by an enabled system user so the system keeps an administrator who can sign in, and `explain(subject, permission)` answers whether a system user or a machine holds one of the system's own permissions, by its short name, and through which grants. An organization user cannot be granted a system role: it is a different identity from any system user, even under the same email.

## An application's tier

An application's store holds its users, its tenants and the definitions it publishes. A user created in the application is an end user of it, in both stores, and a member of its tenant in the application's store, where the tenant sits under the application; a definition is placed under the application in both stores when it is created and taken out when it is deleted. The store holds no default grant, so an application's user granted nothing is refused every request its contract checks.

A definition's rows are checked on the definition the request names, for the permission the entities repository declares for the function:

| Function of `JsonEntitiesRepository` | Permission |
|---|---|
| `findById`, `findByIds`, `findAll`, `search`, `count`, `countByQuery` | `entity_definition_can_read` |
| `namedQuery`, `namedQueryPage` | the permission of the query's statement, below |
| `save`, `bulkSave`, `update`, `bulkUpdate`, `syncIndex` | `entity_definition_can_edit` |
| `deleteById`, `deleteByQuery` | `entity_definition_can_delete` |

A named query is checked, when it is requested, for the permission its statement needs: `entity_definition_can_edit` for an `INSERT`, which replaces a row of the same id, and for an `UPDATE`, `entity_definition_can_delete` for a `DELETE`, and `entity_definition_can_read` for any other statement: a `SELECT`, an aggregate, or a statement a named query may not hold, which the caller then sees refused when it runs. The server reads the statement from the named queries it holds for the definition, so a client never states what its query does. A request naming a definition or a query that does not exist is refused like one the caller lacks the permission for, so a refusal does not tell which queries exist. With `kinotic.debug` on, a refusal names the permission and why the query needs it, such as `the query is an UPDATE`.

The check and the run each read the definition's named queries, so a query redefined between the two runs as redefined: someone who may save the definition's named queries could change a query a viewer is calling from a read into a write at that moment. Saving a definition's named queries needs `entity_definition_can_edit` on the definition, so only an organization member who edits the definition can change a query this way.

`can_delete` implies `can_edit`, which implies `can_read`. `AdminJsonEntitiesRepository` declares for each of its functions the permission `JsonEntitiesRepository` declares for the same one.

A tenant's user is checked on the definition within its tenant, the object `tenant_definition:<definition id>@<tenant id>`, which no store holds tuples for: the authorizer hands the engine its two edges, to the definition and to the tenant, with the check. A grant on that pair, on the definition, on the tenant or on the application all reach the rows, and a grant on the tenant reaches only definitions the application holds. A user with no tenant is checked on the definition itself. A caller above every application, such as a runtime machine or an organization's member, is checked for the same permission on the definition in the system's store, where the definition sits under its application.

A service declared acting across tenants, `@AuthzResource(acrossTenants = true)` on a service of the type `entity_definition`, is checked on the definition itself for every caller, since its functions act on the rows of the tenants a call names: `AdminJsonEntitiesRepository` declares it, so a tenant's user reaches the admin repository only through a grant on the definition or on the application, never through one within its tenant.

The roles of the rows are the same for every definition, `entity_definition.viewer`, `entity_definition.editor` and `entity_definition.admin`, beside `tenant.admin` for everything in a tenant and `application.admin` for everything in the application, together with the roles of the types the application's own services declare.

`ApplicationAccessService`, published in `management-api` for an organization's members, manages an application's grants: `findRoles(applicationId)` lists the roles its store defines, each with the types it may be granted on, `findGrants(applicationId, resource)` the grants that reach a place, each naming where it was made, `grant(applicationId, subject, roleId, resource)` makes one to a user or a machine of the application, named by identity id, on the application, on one of its tenants, on one of its definitions, or on a definition within a tenant, named `<definition id>@<tenant id>`, `revoke(applicationId, resource, grantId)` takes one back, and `explain(applicationId, subject, permission, resource)` answers whether a user holds a permission, by its model name such as `entity_definition_can_read`, and through which grants. A tenant is placed under the application when it is first granted on. Deleting a tenant with `TenantService` takes it out of the store: every grant made on it or on a definition within it, its users' memberships and its place under the application. The application must belong to the caller's organization, a definition named must be one the application holds, and the store has no groups. Reading an application's access needs `application_can_read_access` and managing it `application_can_manage_access`, in the system's store.

### An application's own services

An application's runtime registers each TypeScript service it serves through `ApplicationServiceDirectory.register(entry)`, published in `app-api`, which needs `application_can_register_services` on the entry's application: `application.runtime` bundles it, and every runtime machine a deployment provisions holds that role on its application. The entry carries the definition `kinotic sync` generated with the class's `@AuthzResource` and each function's `@AuthzCheck` or `@AuthzUnchecked`, and the directory derives the checks from it exactly as from a Java interface; the application's store then reconciles to a model carrying the service's type, permissions and roles. A request from one of the application's users to the service is checked in the application's store.

The app server lets a runtime machine subscribe only in the zones of the applications it holds `application_can_register_services` on, read from the engine when it connects (see [CRI Format](/platform/reference/cri-format#zone)), so a runtime hosts its own application's services and no other's.

## In the client

`@kinotic-ai/management-api` mirrors the services as `Kinotic.permissions`, an `IPermissionService`, and `Kinotic.applicationAccess`, an `IApplicationAccessService`, over the models `RoleDefinition`, `Group`, `Grant`, `Subject` with its `SubjectType`, `Resource` and `AccessExplanation`. `@kinotic-ai/system-api` mirrors the system's as `Kinotic.systemAccess`, an `ISystemAccessService` over the same models.

## In the consoles

In the portal, every application and project has an **Access** page in its sidebar, every entity definition an **Access** tab, and Organization settings has **Roles**, **Groups** and **Access** tabs. Each Access panel lists the grants made here and those inherited from above, each naming where it was made, with a **Check access** panel that explains whether a member or a group holds a permission here and through which grants. A member who holds `organization_can_manage_access` sees **Grant access**, which opens a drawer offering a member or a group and the roles whose `grantableOn` names the resource's type, and **Revoke**; everyone else sees the panels without them. Roles are created in a drawer, choosing a level and then permissions of that level and below, and edited there with their level fixed; groups and their members in drawers of their own.

An application's Users page opens its **End-user access** page, and a user's drawer opens it for that one user: the grants in the application's own store on the whole application, on one tenant, on one definition's rows or on those rows in one tenant, a **Grant access** drawer over the application's users and machines, and a check that explains a user's or a machine's access. A page the gateway refuses says so and points back to the list it was opened from.

In the system console, **Members → Access** lists the grants made on the system, with a **Grant access** drawer over the system users and machines, and a check that explains whether one of them holds one of the system's permissions. A system user's drawer on the Users page and a machine's menu on the Machines page open it for that one subject, which is where a vm-manager's machine is granted `vm_node.registrar`.

## Where the data lives

The engine holds the graph: containment, memberships, a role's permissions, a group's members and every grant. Elasticsearch holds the names and descriptions of an organization's own roles and groups, in `kinotic_role` and `kinotic_group`, and the record of each store, in `kinotic_authz_store`.
